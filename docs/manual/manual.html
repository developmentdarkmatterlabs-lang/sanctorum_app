Chapter 1 · Start here

Welcome

Sanctorum is an office of AI agents that you run on your own computer. You build an organisation: floors, teams, roles and the seats inside them. Then you seat agents in it, give them work, and watch them do it.

The idea that sets Sanctorum apart is that an agent's power comes from its seat, not from what it is told. Which tools an agent may use, which folders it may touch and what it may spend all follow from the chair it holds in the organisation. The limits are enforced by the app itself, outside the AI model, so no message can talk an agent past them.

  • Seats grant capability. Each seat holds a role, and each role carries a clearance. The clearance decides the tools: an intern's seat can read, a lead's seat can delegate.
  • Leaders delegate downward. A team's leader can hand pieces of a task to the people in its team, and each of them works at their own clearance, never the leader's.
  • You stay in charge. A supervised agent waits for your approval before every step. An unattended one still stops to ask at its step limit and its cost ceiling.
  • You see everything. Every agent has a live terminal, every task a tree of the runs it started, and every run what it cost.

Sanctorum uses AI models through OpenRouter, with your own key, so you choose the models and pay the providers directly. It starts with a cast of fifteen agents, eleven floors, two teams and a library of over two hundred professional roles, ready to rearrange.

Chapter 2 · Start here

Installing and opening Sanctorum

Sanctorum is a desktop app for Windows and macOS. Everything it needs, including its AI service, is in the installer.

Requirements

WindowsWindows 10 or 11, 64-bit (x64)
macOSmacOS 10.15 Catalina or later, on Apple Silicon (M1 and later) or Intel
InternetNeeded for the AI models, web search and the model list
An OpenRouter keyFrom openrouter.ai/keys, with credit on the account

Installing

Windows

Run Sanctorum-Setup-0.1.0.exe. The installer asks where to install and adds Desktop and Start menu shortcuts. It installs for your user only, so it needs no administrator rights.

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows may show Windows protected your PC. Click More info, then Run anyway.

macOS

Open the disk image for your Mac: arm64 for Apple Silicon, x64 for Intel. Drag Sanctorum onto Applications.

If you downloaded it in a browser, macOS may refuse to open it the first time, because it isn't notarised yet. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway, or right-click the app in Applications and choose Open.

The first time you open it

On the first launch Sanctorum creates your organisation: the floors, the role library and the starting cast. Click Enter the office → to go in.

  1. Open settings… and paste your OpenRouter key under Provider API keys, then click save.
  2. Under Default model, pick the model your agents will think with. It must support tools: look for the tools badge.
  3. Close Settings, click an agent's message, and give it something to do.

Sanctorum keeps your organisation in its own folder on your computer, so uninstalling it doesn't remove your work. See Your data and privacy.

The building

Floors, portals and the floor editor

Sanctorum is a tower. It starts with eleven floors, and every floor has desks where agents sit and a portal that leads to the next floor. Teams have a home floor, and agents walk to their own desks to work.

Managing floors

While you're editing a floor (below), the toolbar shows floors…. Open it to add a floor with + add floor, rename one, or change their order. The portal on each floor leads to the next one in this order, and the last leads back to the first. A floor with agents on it can't be deleted: move them off first.

Editing a floor

Click edit to change the floor you're on. The toolbar switches to the editor's tools:

Removing an agent

Open its dossier, click remove agent at the bottom, and confirm.

Chapter 6 · The organisation

The dossier

Who an agent is, and what it may do

Click an agent's card or its character to open its dossier on the left. The top describes who the agent is. Further down are the settings that decide how it works.

Identity

The portrait, title and tagline are the agent's own. So is its Profession, but the profession is only a label. It changes nothing about what the agent may do.

Clearance is read-only, because it comes from the seat the agent holds. The dossier says where it comes from, for example inherited from role "Engineering Lead". To change an agent's clearance, give it a different seat, or change the seat or its role in Teams.

Data type is the other half: clearance decides what an agent may do, and data type what it may know. An agent only reads memory entries at or below its own data type. Tenure and Focus describe the agent.

Floor and Seat choose its desk, and the position list seats it in a team, or puts it on the bench with no position.

How it works

SettingWhat it decides
ModelThe AI model this agent thinks with. Click choose… to pick one; leave it on the global default otherwise. It must support tools.
Workspace directoryBlank, the agent works in a folder Sanctorum keeps for its floor, shared with the agents there. Set a real folder on your computer and it works there instead, read-only unless you untick that.
PersonalityThe voice it speaks in. See Personalities.
Web accessText only, the browser, or both, overriding the global setting. See The web.
SupervisionTicked, the agent asks your approval before every tool it uses. Unticked, it works unattended, still inside its clearance and workspace.
Steps per runHow many steps it may take without checking in with you. Blank uses the global setting.

Giving an agent write access to a real folder lets it create and change your files there. Only allow it for a folder you intend the agent to change.

Leaders

An agent in a leader seat with clearance 6 or more can delegate, and gets two more settings:

  • Let this leader delegate without asking skips your approval for handing out work. Every other step still follows its supervision setting, and all the limits still apply.
  • Budget (USD) caps what this leader and everything it delegates may spend on one task. The global cost ceiling still applies, and the lower of the two wins.

Below these, the dossier lists the Rules in effect for the seat, its Skills and MCP servers, and the agent's Responsibilities.

Chapter 7 · The organisation

Teams and seats

A team is a group of seats with a mission. Each seat holds a role, the role gives the seat its clearance, and the agent sitting in it works with that clearance. Open teams… to see them.

A team

  • Home floor: where the team works.
  • Reports into: the team above it, or top level. The leader of the team above may delegate work to this team's leader.
  • Mission: one line on what the team is for.

Seats

Each row is a seat:

  • The role it holds.
  • Its clearance: inherit takes the role's, shown in brackets, or you can pick a level for this seat alone.
  • The star makes the seat the team's leader. A team has one leader, marked LEADER.
  • assign seats an agent; the cross beside its name unseats it.
  • The last cross removes the seat.

To add a seat, choose a role under add a seat for… and click + add. To start a team, fill in Name and Mission at the bottom and click create.

Team memory

Below its seats, each team shows its shared memory: what its agents have written down, and anything you add yourself. Type in Add a memory…, choose a Kind and a minimum clearance to read it (Clr), and click add. See Memory.

Roles

A role is a job, defined once and shared by every seat that holds it. Sanctorum starts with a library of over two hundred professional roles in disciplines from Engineering and Data to Finance, Legal and Writing. Open roles… to browse them.

The list shows each role's clearance and how many seats hold it. Search by title, discipline or description, and click a role to edit it:

FieldMeaning
TitleThe job's name, shown on every seat that holds it.
DisciplineThe group it's listed under. Type a new name to start a new group.
Level (band)Its seniority, for your own reference.
Default clearanceThe clearance every seat holding this role inherits.
DescriptionWhat the job is responsible for.

Editing a role changes every seat that holds it at once, except seats that chose their own clearance. That's the point: raise a role's clearance, and everyone in that job gets the new tools on their next task.

Click + new to add a role. A role held by seats can't be deleted until those seats hold something else.

Chapter 10 · The organisation

Skills, rules and MCP servers

Three more things shape how agents work. Skills and MCP servers belong to seats, like clearance. Standing rules apply by scope, to everyone they cover.

Skills

A skill is a playbook for a kind of task: when asked to review code, follow these steps. Open skills…, click + new, and give it a Name, a one-line Description, a Minimum clearance, and its Body, the steps themselves, which are given to the agent with every task.

Skills live on seats. Grant one in an agent's dossier, under Skills (on this seat); whoever holds the seat has it.

Standing rules

A standing rule is how your organisation always works: always answer in English, never delete files without asking. Rules are given to every run they cover, ahead of the role and the skills.

  • Scope: global for every agent, team for one team, or position for one seat. Choose which under Applies to.
  • Order sets where it appears among the others.
  • Enabled: untick to pause a rule without deleting it.

Each agent's dossier lists the rules in effect for its seat. The rules for one seat are capped at about 4,000 characters; past that, the most specific rules are dropped first, so global rules always apply.

MCP servers

MCP servers give agents tools from outside Sanctorum: editing Office documents, querying a database, working with another service. Open mcps…, click + new, and choose the Transport:

  • http (remote): the Server URL, and the access token if it needs one.
  • stdio (local, advanced): the Command that starts the server on your computer, and its Args.

Set a Min clearance, then grant the server on a seat in an agent's dossier, under MCPs (on this seat). Its tools follow the same approvals as Sanctorum's own. If a server can't be reached, the run carries on without it.

Access tokens for MCP servers are stored on your computer without encryption, unlike your provider keys, and are never shown again after you save them.

Trying a local server

A good first server is the free everything test server, which has simple tools such as echo. It needs Node.js on your computer.

  1. Open mcps… and click + new.
  2. Enter the Name everything and choose the Transport stdio (local, advanced).
  3. Enter the Command npx and the Args ["-y","@modelcontextprotocol/server-everything"]. Args are a list in square brackets, each part in quotes.
  4. Leave Min clearance at 0, keep Enabled ticked, and save.
  5. Open the dossier of an agent that holds a seat, and add everything under MCPs (on this seat).
  6. Give the agent a task such as Use the echo tool to say hello.

The agent's terminal shows how it went:

LineMeaning
MCP tools available: echo, …The server started, and the agent can use its tools.
MCP server 'everything' skipped: …The server couldn't start or be reached. The rest of the line says why; the task carries on without it.
MCP tool 'echo' on server 'everything' failed: …One call went wrong. The agent is told and can try another way.

A supervised agent pauses before each call to the server, as for any other tool. A call that takes longer than two minutes is stopped, and the agent is told.

Sanctorum introduces itself to each server by name, so an app that lists or asks to allow connected apps shows it as Sanctorum.

A local server runs on your computer with your user account. It doesn't get your provider keys, but it can see where your user folders are. Only add servers you trust.

Connecting another app

Many apps can be worked by an AI assistant through MCP: design tools, 3D programs, music plugins, online services. You connect each one the same way.

  1. Find the app's MCP setup. Look in its settings for MCP, AI assistant or Connect Claude Desktop. Most apps show a short block of setup text meant for Claude Desktop or another AI app. Sanctorum isn't listed there, but the same details work.
  2. Turn the setup into Sanctorum's fields. A local app's setup usually looks like this: 
    "my-app": {
  "command": "C:\\Program Files\\MyApp\\my-app-helper.exe",
  "args": ["stdio"]
}
    In mcps…, click + new, choose stdio (local, advanced), and copy across:
    • Name: the name before the colon, here my-app.
    • Command: the command, written with single backslashes: C:\Program Files\MyApp\my-app-helper.exe. The setup text doubles them only because of how it's written. Spaces in the path are fine.
    • Args: the args exactly as shown, square brackets and quotes included: ["stdio"].
    If the setup gives a url instead of a command, the app is online: choose http (remote), paste the address into Server URL, and paste its access token, if it gives you one.
  3. Save, and grant it to a seat. In an agent's dossier, add it under MCPs (on this seat). A supervised agent is a good choice the first time, so you approve each call.
  4. Open the app. Many apps answer only while they're running, with the project you want to work on open.
  5. Start with a task that only reads. For example: List what you can do in my-app, then tell me what's in the current project. The terminal's MCP tools available: … line lists the app's tools by name.
  6. Allow Sanctorum in the app, if it asks. Some apps ask before a new AI app connects, and list it under a name such as connected apps. Allow Sanctorum, then send the task again. Until you do, the agent is told the app is waiting for you.
  7. Then ask for changes. If the app can show changes as a preview to accept or reject, keep that on while you get to know the agent.

When the result of a task is a change inside another app, write your own success criteria in the agent's chat, such as the change is made in the app and the agent confirms it was applied. Otherwise the agent may be graded on how it describes the change, and spend extra steps redrawing its answer.

If you seeTry
MCP server '…' skipped: FileNotFoundErrorThe Command path is wrong. Copy it again from the app's setup.
skipped with another reasonMake sure the app is open. For an online app, check the address and the token.
The app asks you to allow it, or the agent says it is waiting for accessAllow Sanctorum in the app's settings, then send the task again.
timed out after 120 sThe app took too long, or was waiting for you to click something in its own window.
Chapter 11 · Work

Giving an agent a task

Messages, the terminal and grading

Click an agent's message to open your conversation with it in the inbox, type what you want in Tell them what to do…, and send it. The agent walks to its desk and starts.

Success criteria

Every task is graded. Before it starts, the agent turns your task into one testable criterion, shown with 🎯 in the conversation. When it believes it's done, a second check grades the answer against that criterion. If the answer falls short, the agent tries again with the feedback, up to three attempts. Each grade appears with ⚖.

To set the criterion yourself, open 🎯 success criteria under the message box before sending. Blank, it's worked out from the task.

The terminal

Click >_ on an agent's card to watch it work. The terminal shows every line of its tasks as they happen:

LineMeaning
picked up the taskThe run started, with its clearance and the tools it was granted.
$ write_file …A step: a tool the agent is using.
Grey textWhat the step returned.
🎯 and ⚖The success criterion, and each grade.
⏸The agent is waiting for your decision.
White textThe agent's answer.

While a task is running, type in the terminal's box to interrupt and redirect: the current run stops and the agent starts on your new instruction. The stop button ends the run without a new one.

Chapter 12 · Work

Approvals and limits

Sanctorum never lets an agent run away from you. How often it asks depends on its supervision setting.

Supervised agents

A supervised agent stops before every tool it uses and shows ⏸ approve step with the step it wants to take, in its terminal, in your conversation with it, and as a waiting light in the title bar.

ChoiceWhat happens
proceedThe step runs, and the agent carries on to the next one.
editYou write a revised instruction instead. The proposed step is cancelled and the agent re-plans from what you wrote.
stopThe task ends.

Unattended agents

An agent with supervision off works on its own, but it still stops to ask in two cases:

The step limit

After a number of steps without a check-in, the run pauses with reached 200 steps without a check-in. Proceed to allow 200 more. Proceed and it carries on for another batch; any approval restarts the count. Set the number for everyone in Settings, under Steps per run, or for one agent in its dossier. A step is one round of tool use, and an agent sometimes uses several tools in a single step.

The cost ceiling

Once a task has spent its cost ceiling, the run pauses with cost ceiling reached ($2.01 of $2.00 for this task). Proceed to keep going. If you proceed, it won't ask about cost again for the rest of that run. Delegating new work past the ceiling stays refused.

A pause loses nothing. The run keeps its place while it waits, and continues from the same step when you decide.

Chapter 13 · Work

Delegation

A team's leader can split a task and hand the pieces to its reports. This is how one request becomes the work of a whole team.

Who may delegate to whom

  • Only an agent in a leader seat with clearance 6 or more can delegate.
  • It delegates downward: to the seats in its own team, and to the leaders of teams that report into its team. Never up, never sideways.
  • Each report works at its own clearance. A leader can't lend its powers: a task needing a tool the report lacks is refused, and the leader has to give it to someone who can do it.

Several reports can work at once. After handing out the work, the leader waits for their reports, reviews them, and either accepts them or sends work back with notes.

The delegation tree

Every task with delegation forms a tree of runs. Open it from the tree button on the leader's tile in the fleet… view. For each run it shows who did it, its state, the tokens it used and how many came from the provider's cache, and what it cost. The header totals the task against its limits.

A report waiting for your approval shows its step in the tree, with proceed and stop beside it. stop all ends the leader and every report under it.

Limits

LimitWhereWhat it bounds
DepthSettingsHow many levels down work can be passed, from 1 (no delegation) to 10.
Runs per taskSettingsHow many runs one task may start in total. 0 means no limit.
Cost ceilingSettingsWhat one task may spend, in US dollars. 0 means no limit.
BudgetA leader's dossierWhat that leader and its reports may spend on one task.

A task keeps the limits it started with, so changing them in Settings affects the next task, not the one running.

Chapter 14 · Work

Memory

Agents keep notes between tasks: decisions made, conventions agreed, what went wrong last time. They read their memory before starting work, and agents with clearance 3 or more write new entries when they learn something worth keeping.

Three scopes

ScopeWho sees it
AgentOnly that agent. Its private notes.
PositionWhoever holds the seat. It stays with the seat when the agent changes.
TeamEveryone on the team. Shown and edited in Teams.

Kinds and protection

Each entry is a task, a result, an insight or a note. It also carries a minimum clearance, so a junior seat on the team can't read what's meant for senior ones, and a data type, so an agent only reads entries at or below its own data type.

If an entry you added disappears from a team's memory view, its minimum clearance is probably above what the view's seat holds. The entry is still there; the protection is working.

Chapter 15 · Work

The web, images and speech

Searching and reading the web

Agents search the web with Serper, so web search needs a Serper key in Settings. To read a page they have two ways, chosen under Web access in Settings, or per agent in its dossier:

Web accessHow pages are read
Text onlyThe page is fetched and read as text, with no browser. Cheap and fast, but sites that build themselves with JavaScript may come back empty. From clearance 1.
BrowserThe agent opens pages in a real browser that you can watch in the app, clicking, typing and scrolling like a person. Works on any site, at roughly ten times the cost. From clearance 5.
BothThe model picks for each page.

Each agent has its own browser, which stays signed in to the sites it uses between tasks. Click sign out in its browser pane to clear them all. For your safety, agents can't open addresses on your own computer or your local network.

An agent signed in to a site can act as you there. Pages, files and search results can also contain instructions aimed at the agent. Its clearance and your approvals still hold, but keep agents that browse unfamiliar sites supervised, or at a low clearance.

Images and speech

From clearance 3, agents can create images and audio, saved into their workspace. Choose the models in Settings, under Image model and Speech model, or per agent. Both are billed by OpenRouter like any other call.

Chapter 16 · Work

The fleet and announcements

The fleet

Open fleet… to see every agent as a tile: its title, what it's doing, and the last line of its latest task. A tile waiting for you shows the step with proceed, edit and stop, and a working tile has a box to redirect it. open terminal opens its terminal, and tree opens the delegation tree of its current task.

Announcements

Sanctorum can tell you when an agent replies or finishes, so you don't have to watch. Choose under Announce in Settings:

  • Off: silent.
  • Chime: a short sound.
  • Voice: your computer reads out who finished, in the voice you pick.

Announcements use your computer's own voices: free, instant and offline. Test plays a sample. The choice is kept on this computer only.

Chapter 19 · Settings and reference

What it costs

Sanctorum itself is free. The AI models are paid for through your OpenRouter account, by the token: a token is a piece of a word, and each call is charged for what it reads in and what it writes out. Prices differ a lot between models, and the model picker shows them.

Where to see it

  • The title bar shows what the current task has spent so far.
  • The delegation tree shows each run's tokens in and out, how many were read from the provider's cache, and its cost.

Why long tasks cost more

An agent's whole conversation is sent to the model again at every step, so a long task reads more with each step it takes. Providers cache the repeated part, and bill it at a fraction of the price: Sanctorum asks for this on Claude models, where it's about a tenth, and most other providers do it on their own.

Spending less

  • Give simple seats a cheaper model in their dossiers.
  • Keep Web access on Text only where the browser isn't needed: a browsed page costs roughly ten times more.
  • Leave Skip repeat reads on.
  • Set a Cost ceiling, and a Budget on leaders that delegate a lot.
  • Give big jobs as several smaller tasks, so each conversation stays short.
Chapter 20 · Settings and reference

Your data and privacy

What stays on your computer

Your organisation, conversations, run history, memory and settings are kept in Sanctorum's folder: %APPDATA%\Sanctorum on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Sanctorum on macOS. Agents' workspaces, uploaded portraits and each agent's browser profile live there too. Before an update changes the database, Sanctorum saves a copy of it beside the original.

Uninstalling Sanctorum leaves this folder in place, so reinstalling finds your organisation intact. Delete it yourself to start over, and back it up to keep your work safe.

What leaves it

  • Each run sends OpenRouter what the model needs: the agent's instructions, your task and the conversation so far, including the contents of files it read and pages it visited. OpenRouter passes it to the maker of the model you chose.
  • Searches go to Serper; pages are requested from the sites themselves; the browser visits sites like any browser.
  • MCP servers you add receive the calls agents make to them.

Sanctorum sends no usage analytics, telemetry or crash reports, has no account, and doesn't check for updates on its own. The full privacy policy is published with Sanctorum's source code.

Chapter 21 · Settings and reference

Tips and troubleshooting

"The model does not support tool use"

The chosen model can't use tools, and every seated agent needs them. Pick one with the tools badge, in Settings or in the agent's dossier.

"The OpenRouter account has no credit left"

Add credit to your OpenRouter account, then send the task again.

"No provider is currently serving this model"

The model is temporarily unavailable on OpenRouter. Choose another one.

An agent refuses to use a tool

The tool is above its seat's clearance. Check the clearance in its dossier, and remember that an agent without a seat has none. See Clearance reference.

An agent won't open the browser

The browser needs clearance 5 and Web access set to Browser or Both.

Nothing seems to happen

Look for the waiting light in the title bar: an agent may be waiting for your approval, at a step, its step limit or its cost ceiling. Answer it in its terminal, in the inbox, or in the delegation tree for a report.

Windows says it protected your PC, or macOS won't open the app

Sanctorum isn't code-signed yet. See Installing and opening Sanctorum.

On macOS, the app opens from Applications but not from the Dock

The Dock icon may still point at an old copy. Remove it from the Dock, open Sanctorum from Applications, and keep that icon in the Dock instead.
